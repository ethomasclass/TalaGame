The idea here

When one migrant helps the next one come over — a ticket, a place to sleep, a job already waiting — geographers call it chain migration. It is why whole families end up on one block.

17

You chose today or in three days. Which, and what did that choice cost the family?

The idea here

Money that workers send home to family in another country is called a remittance. Filipinos abroad send home around thirty-four billion dollars a year — close to a tenth of everything the Philippine economy produces. It arrives four hundred dollars at a time.

18

Pa was a licensed pharmacist in the Philippines for eleven years. What is his job now, and why can’t he do his old one here?

The idea here

When a country loses trained people to other countries, that is brain drain. When those people arrive and end up doing work far below their training, that is brain waste. Pa is both at once.

3 · 4 · 5

Batangas, in the Philippines; two years in Jersey City. Simbang Gabi is nine dawn masses, 16–24 December; finish all nine and you are said to get one wish. Ma was asked publicly, by someone who invoked her own mother’s reputation — refusing would have cost her standing in the one community she has here. Accept any answer naming social pressure or obligation.

6 · 7

Answers vary. The point is scarcity: thirty minutes buys two stops, or three if they took the chain store. Watch for students who notice that choosing the neighbourhood store cost them a stop — that is the whole mechanic and it is question 9’s setup.

8 · 9

Bayanihan Cargo (shipping boxes home), Mang Boy’s grocery, the panaderia, the salon — all selling mainly to Filipino families; Quik Mart sells to anyone. Reasons to still use Mang Boy’s: he carries the exact cane vinegar, he extends credit, he knows the family, he asks about Lola. Trust and credit are the strongest answers.

10 · 11

Chain migration — the family paid toward his ticket and a job was already waiting because he is family. Ando speaks English on purpose, to get ready; nobody asked him to. Good answers notice he is not a simpler person in English, he is just working in his second language.

12 · 13 · 14

Ma was speaking English the whole time. The customer was reacting to her accent, not her language — that is discrimination or xenophobia, and the scene is built so the excuse does not hold. Ma and Pa switch to Tagalog not to hide the meaning (Tala grew up in it) but to mark the conversation as not hers.

15

Cultural change under market pressure — acculturation. Every dish changed to sell better moves the menu further from Lola’s card. Strong answers name the trade: survive as a business, or keep the food exact.

16 · 17

$400 sent; fee $14 (same day) or $5 (three days); rate $1 = 52.40 PHP; 20,960 pesos reach Lola; $414 or $405 leaves the register; arrives that night or on the 26th. The cost of speed is nine dollars; the cost of saving nine dollars is three days with a hospital deposit outstanding.

18

He cooks. His Philippine licence does not transfer, and requalifying means years of school and exams the family cannot pay for now. Brain waste. Push them on the debrief version: the US did not gain a pharmacist.

19 · 20

Push: no work for her at home, the clinic understaffed since August, family needing money. Pull: far higher pay, a real nursing job, a programme that accepted her. When she goes, the town loses its second nurse and has no one to replace her — the shortage gets worse because the training worked.

21

The enclave is a two-way safety net, not only an outbound pipe. Best answers notice Ma’s discomfort: the help is real and it is public, so everyone now knows they needed it.

23

No correct side. Full marks need a real reason for their side and a fair statement of the other: remittances pay the hospital bill and keep the lights on, and they also fund the training that removes the trained person. Both are true at once, which is the actual lesson.
