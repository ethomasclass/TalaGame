How Real Should Tala Be?

Three steps toward realism from where the game is now. Same girl, same straight-on pose, same warm light from the parol on her left and cool light from the window on her right. Everything here is plain JavaScript drawing on a canvas — no libraries, nothing a school laptop can’t run offline.

Steps 1–3 breathe, blink and glance around.

What realism costs that isn’t the face

  • The world has to come up with her. A painted Tala standing in today’s flat restaurant looks cut out and pasted in. Every step up means repainting twelve rooms, not just nine faces — that’s the real bill.
  • Realism raises the stakes on who she looks like. At step 0 nobody reads her features as a statement about Filipino faces. At step 3 everyone does. The review by a Filipino colleague goes from useful to essential, ideally with reference photos of the family you picture.
  • Expressions get harder to fake. At step 0 a frown is two lines. At step 3 a frown has to move the brow, the lid and the shading on the cheek together, or it looks wrong.
  • There’s a ceiling on hand-coded faces. Past step 2 you’d want a human illustrator painting the portraits and the code animating them — which is already how this page works: each portrait is painted once, and only the eyes and the breathing are live.

What I’d do

Step 2. “Slightly more realistic” lands here: she reads as a real person, it stays warm, and it’s buildable across seven characters and twelve rooms.
